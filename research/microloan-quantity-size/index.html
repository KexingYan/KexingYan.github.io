The Quantity and Size of Microloans Follow Different Development Logics: Evidence from Kiva

This paper studies how Kiva activity relates to economic development at three distinct margins: whether Kiva operates in a country, total lending volume conditional on entry, and the size of an individual loan.

Abstract

Using Kiva loan records and World Bank indicators, the paper tests whether microfinance activity follows an inverted-U, or “middle-income peak,” as countries develop. It applies the same formal shape-test standard to country entry, total lending volume, and individual loan size. The results differ across these margins: total volume does not show significant nonlinearity; individual loan size is U-shaped; and country entry is significantly concave, but its initial rise among the poorest countries is not statistically distinguishable from flat.

Research question and motivation

The paper asks where platform-based microfinance is most active as countries develop, and whether the answer changes when activity is measured as country entry, aggregate lending, or individual loan size. Separating the three outcomes avoids treating entry into a market and lending intensity within that market as the same object.

Data

The main Kiva-active panel covers 84 countries from 2013 through 2017. Loan-level Kiva posting records are combined with World Bank indicators for GDP per capita, population, governance, institutions, and financial access. A separate global panel includes zero-lending country-years so the country-entry margin can be studied directly.

The public code repository retains processed analytical datasets and documents the construction workflow. It does not include the large raw Kiva loan files or a pinned software environment, so a clean clone is not sufficient for complete end-to-end reproduction.

Methodology

The analysis estimates country-year and loan-level OLS models with clustered standard errors, a Logit model for whether Kiva is active in a country-year, and PPML models for total lending when true zeros are included. Institutional quality is summarized with principal component analysis. Quadratic specifications are assessed with Lind–Mehlum intersection-union tests and turning-point confidence intervals rather than relying on the sign of a squared term alone.

Key findings

  1. Country entry: Kiva activity is significantly concave in development and falls steeply across richer countries, but the formal test does not establish a complete inverted-U because the low-end rise is not statistically confirmed.
  2. Total lending volume, conditional on entry: the estimated quadratic term is negative but not statistically significant in the preferred specification (p = 0.179), so the paper does not claim a middle-income peak in volume.
  3. Individual loan size: the preferred loan-level model finds a positive quadratic term (p < 0.001), and the formal test confirms a U-shape within the observed range.

Interpretation and contribution

These findings describe different outcomes rather than competing estimates of one relationship. A country can become less likely to receive any Kiva lending as it develops while the total volume and typical size of loans made there follow different patterns. The paper's methodological contribution is to decompose those margins and apply the same formal test to every proposed nonlinear shape.

Limitations

The evidence is observational and should be interpreted as conditional association, not causation. The Kiva-active panels are selected on platform entry; the global model addresses part of that selection but does not identify why Kiva enters particular countries. The panel spans only five years, and some of the loan-size pattern may reflect local price or income levels. Loan-level findings also remain conditional on a loan having been made.
